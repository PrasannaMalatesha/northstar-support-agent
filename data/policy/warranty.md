# Warranty

The return window applies first. Warranty time starts after that window. Lengths live only in WAR-COVERAGE.

## WAR-COVERAGE

**Rule**: If a manufacturing defect is reported inside the return window in REF-CATEGORY, handle it as a return, not as a warranty claim. Warranty coverage begins the day after that window ends. From that day, electronics, home and kitchen, and bags and accessories are covered for 1 year against manufacturing defects. Apparel and footwear are covered for 90 days against manufacturing defects. A covered defect is a fault in materials or assembly that appears in ordinary use.

**Applies when**: A customer reports a defect, and the specialist needs to know whether the return window or the warranty applies.

**Agent must not**: Start the warranty on the delivery date, or quote a length that is not in this section.

**Example**: A speaker's return window has ended. A defect reported the next day is a warranty claim, covered for 1 year from that next day. The draft cites WAR-COVERAGE.

## WAR-EXCLUSIONS

**Rule**: The warranty does not cover normal wear, cuts, stains, misuse, or damage caused after delivery. It does not cover final-sale lines. It does not cover a change of mind. Those follow the returns handbook.

**Applies when**: The damage described is wear, misuse, or a change of mind.

**Agent must not**: Open a warranty claim for a stain, a cut, or a customer who simply dislikes the item.

**Example**: A coat sleeve is stained after a month of wear. That is not a manufacturing defect. The draft cites WAR-EXCLUSIONS.

## WAR-CLAIM

**Rule**: A warranty claim needs the order id, the line, a short description of the defect, and a delivery date that still falls inside WAR-COVERAGE. If any of those are missing, ask for them. If coverage has ended, deny the claim under WAR-COVERAGE. The specialist drafts the claim. A person submits it. The agent does not tell the customer the claim is approved.

**Applies when**: The defect is outside the return window and sounds like a manufacturing defect.

**Agent must not**: Approve the claim in the customer draft, or invent a missing order id.

**Example**: The order id and the defect description are present, and coverage has not ended. The draft collects nothing further and says a person will submit the claim. It cites WAR-CLAIM.

## WAR-REMEDY

**Rule**: The warranty remedy is repair, replacement of the same item, or a refund of that line, in that order. Replacement follows REP-REPLACEMENT when the catalog row says in stock. If it is not in stock and repair is not available, the remedy is a refund of the line. The remedy waits for a person before it is recorded.

**Applies when**: A warranty claim has the facts WAR-CLAIM requires.

**Agent must not**: Promise a replacement that is not in stock, or record the remedy before a person approves it.

**Example**: A covered kettle is in stock. The proposal is a replacement. The draft cites WAR-REMEDY and does not say the replacement has shipped.

## WAR-RECALL

**Rule**: A safety recall always escalates under ESC-WHEN and is refunded in full, including outbound shipping, regardless of REF-WINDOW or WAR-COVERAGE. The agent does not decide that a recall exists unless the handbook or the order record says so.

**Applies when**: The case names a recall that the handbook or the order record already identifies.

**Agent must not**: Declare a recall from general knowledge, or refuse a recall refund because the return window has ended.

**Example**: The order record flags the line as recalled. The proposal is a full refund and an escalation. The draft cites WAR-RECALL.
